# What is Sitrep?

Imagine a big wall screen in a movie where someone watches the whole world at
once — blinking dots on a map, news headlines rolling past, numbers ticking.
**Sitrep is that wall screen, in a web page, built only from free public
information.**

The name comes from the military word for "situation report" — a short
summary of what's happening right now.

## What you see when you open it

- **A world map** with colored dots: red for conflict-ish news, amber for
  disasters like earthquakes and storms, plus rings around places where
  several different sources are reporting at once (that's usually where the
  interesting stuff is).
- **A news feed** on the left that you can filter by topic or by your own
  keywords.
- **A short brief** on the right that reads like a newsroom summary of the
  moment.
- **A small market strip** — prices often react to world events first.

## Where the information comes from

Public sources anyone could read: the US Geological Survey (earthquakes),
NASA (storms, fires, volcanoes), a UN/EU disaster-alert system, international
news outlets, and GDELT — a huge public crawl of world news. Sitrep just puts
them on one screen.

## What it is NOT

- Not a spy tool and not secret — everything is already public.
- Not a crystal ball — dots show what sources *reported*, not what will happen.
- Not the final word — always click through to the original story before
  believing or sharing anything.

## If you're just curious

Open it, click dots, read headlines. If you're more technical, Sitrep is also
free open-source software you can run yourself or feed to an AI assistant —
the other doc sets explain how.
