# Reading the Map

> **Last updated:** 2026-10-19 · **Authors:** mridun-gupta & Devin (AI-assisted)


## The dots

Every dot is something that happened (or was reported) at that place. Bigger
dot = more serious, roughly speaking:

- **Gray/blue** — small or routine
- **Amber** — worth noticing
- **Orange** — serious
- **Red** — the most severe category

Color tells you *what kind* of thing it is: red for conflict, amber for
natural disasters, purple for humanitarian alerts, green for environment,
yellow for military aircraft, teal for health, and so on. The layer buttons
at the top-left let you turn groups on and off.

## The bunches

When lots of events pile into one area they group into a circle with a
number. Click the circle to zoom in and spread them out.

## The amber rings (hotspots)

A ring means *different, unrelated sources* are reporting about the same area
at the same time — e.g., a satellite spots a wildfire AND a UN alert goes out
AND news turns sharply negative there. When independent sources agree, that's
usually a real signal, so Sitrep draws attention to it.

## The glow (tension)

The soft warm glow shows where the news is loudest and gloomiest — places
generating lots of negative coverage. Think of it as a media "heat map", not
a danger map.

## The pin that isn't quite where you expected

If a headline mentions a country but no specific city, the dot sits at the
country's center — so a pin in the middle of France means "news said
'France'", not "something happened in the Massif Central".

## One honest warning

Every dot is a *report*, not a verified fact. Click it to read the original
source before drawing conclusions.
